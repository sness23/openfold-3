# Key Quotes from AlQuraishi's Blog Posts

*For use in YouTube video about OpenFold3*

These two blog posts are the narrative backbone of the video. They trace AlQuraishi's journey from shocked observer (2018) to frustrated critic (2020) to builder of the open-source alternative (2025).

---

## Post 1: "AlphaFold @ CASP13: What just happened?" (December 9, 2018)

Source: https://moalquraishi.wordpress.com/2018/12/09/alphafold-casp13-what-just-happened/

### The Opening

> "'What just happened?' was a question put to me in exactly these words by at least one researcher at CASP."

### The Irony

> "In delicious irony, those who bet careers on obsoleting crystallographers now worry about getting obsoleted themselves."

Great line — self-aware, biting, captures the recursive anxiety of scientific progress.

### The Stages of Grief

> "Many researchers went through these phases: (i) fearing DeepMind outsmarted everyone via brilliant fundamental insight combined with virtuoso engineering; (ii) breathing relief that insights were not radically different from field thinking; (iii) slightly belittling DeepMind's contribution by noting seeming incrementality and crediting success to Alphabet's resources."

Brutally honest description of how the community processed the shock.

### The Central Indictment

> "What just happened represents a serious academic science indictment, particularly regarding protein structure prediction. Dozens of academic groups, with researchers likely numbering in the low hundreds, work on this problem. Decades of work with vast expertise... Yet DeepMind's group of approximately ten researchers, primarily (though not exclusively) with ML expertise, thoroughly routed everyone."

The passage that made the blog post famous.

### The Pharma Demolition

> "What is worse than academic groups getting scooped by DeepMind? The fact that collective Novartis, Pfizer, etc. powers, with hundreds of thousands (roughly a million) employees, let an industrial lab that is a complete field outsider, with virtually no prior molecular sciences experience, enter and thoroughly beat them on a problem of far greater importance to pharmaceuticals than Alphabet."

Then escalates:

> "They are clueless, rudderless, and asleep at the helm."

### The Role Reversal

> "Consider this counterfactual: Take a problem proximal to tech companies' bottom line, e.g. image recognition or speech, and imagine no tech company invested research money into it... Then imagine a pharmaceutical company suddenly enters ImageNet and blows competition away... Does this seem realistic? Absolutely not."

Makes the absurdity viscerally clear.

### The Gracious Ending

> "First and foremost, recognize what an unqualifiable good thing just happened. The entire scientific community has made a major advance on one of biochemistry's most important problems. Who made the advance matters less than that it was made, and we should unselfishly rejoice."

Then the emotional honesty:

> "I say this cognizant that personal emotions do not entirely coincide with this sentiment, but also cognizant that we are all adults who must assess this rationally without letting tribal affiliations cloud judgment."

Admitting his own feelings don't match his rational position is what makes the whole post resonate.

### On Simple Ideas

> "The best science is where simple ideas have profound consequences, and it very much appears the case here."

## Post 2: "AlphaFold2 @ CASP14: It feels like one's child has left home" (December 8, 2020)

Source: https://moalquraishi.wordpress.com/2020/12/08/alphafold2-casp14-it-feels-like-ones-child-has-left-home/

### The Reluctant Sequel

> "I struggled with whether I should write this blog post as it felt at times like an obligation rather than something I desired to do. Sequels are also never as good as the original and the weight of expectation from my CASP13 post fazed me."

Different, heavier tone. Two years of processing.

### The Declaration

> "At CASP14 DeepMind produced an advance so thorough it compelled CASP organizers to declare the protein structure prediction problem for single protein chains to be solved."

The weight is in the simplicity.

### The Prediction That Came True Too Fast

> "I expected us to get to a median of 4 angstrom Ca RMSD by 2022, which if you look at where the next best group is, would be about right. Instead we got 1.6 angstrom in 2020."

His own 2018 predictions were accurate for the field — but not for DeepMind.

### The Title Quote

> "This was captured poignantly by a panelist at the very last session of the conference who remarked that CASP14 feels a bit like when one's child leaves home for the very first time. It is good, in the sense that they have now matured, as has the field, and are ready to tackle bigger and better problems. But there is a bittersweetness to the experience that cannot be ignored."

The emotional centerpiece. Perfect for voiceover.

### The Field is Shattered

> "The core field has been blown to pieces; there's just no sugar-coating it."

### Personal Impact

> "In some ways negatively affected on a personal level as AlphaFold2 essentially obsoletes at least parts of our research programs."

Critical for the narrative — this is the man who would go on to build OpenFold. His research was "obsoleted" and he chose to rebuild rather than retreat.

### THE PIVOT QUOTE — The Critique of Secrecy

> "What was presented on AF2 at CASP14 barely resembled a methods talk. It was exceedingly high-level, heavy on ideas and insinuations but almost entirely devoid of detail. This is a shame and contrasts markedly with DeepMind's participation in CASP13... While at CASP13 I and many others were surprised by DM's entry and impressed by their results, we all walked away feeling like there's a great new group of colleagues in the community. I'm afraid this time they left a different impression."

> **"Withholding details stands to poison the well of goodwill in the community."**

**THIS is the founding manifesto of OpenFold.** The frustration with secrecy around a solved problem of immense biological importance is what motivated AlQuraishi to build an open reproduction. For the video, this is the hinge point — where observer becomes builder.

### A Thousand Flowers

> "The fast and focused model minimizes the percolation and exploration of ideas. Instead of a thousand flowers blooming, only one will, and it may prevent future bloomings by stripping them of perceived academic novelty."

Beautiful articulation of what's lost when one entity solves a problem so completely.

### The Structural Advantage

> "There is a reason that the AF2 abstract has 18 co-first authors and it is reflective of an incentive structure wholly foreign to academia. Research at universities is ultimately about individual effort and building a personal brand... This means the power of coordination that DeepMind can leverage is never available to academic groups."

Diagnosis of the structural problem, not just the scientific one.

### 90/10

> "Protein structure prediction is at the 90% of the problem but 10% of the effort stage."

## The Narrative Arc: Observer → Critic → Builder

| Year | Role | Key Quote |
|------|------|-----------|
| **2018** | Shocked observer | "What just happened?" |
| **2018** | Gracious analyst | "We should unselfishly rejoice" |
| **2020** | Wounded competitor | "Obsoletes at least parts of our research programs" |
| **2020** | Open science advocate | "Withholding details stands to poison the well" |
| **2020** | Mourning a field | "The core field has been blown to pieces" |
| **2021–2024** | Builder | Leads OpenFold (AF2 reproduction), then OpenFold3 |
| **2025–2026** | Vindicated | OpenFold3 matches AF3 on RNA, Apache 2.0, full training stack released |

The unwritten third blog post is OpenFold3 itself.
